MAC Address

  • Media Access Control Address (MAC Address)
  • Unique identifier for a given NIC (Network Interface Controller)
  • 48 bit (6 byte) value
  • 2^48 (281,474,976,710,656) possible values
  • A MAC address may include the manufacturer's OUI (organizationally unique identifier)
  • MAC addresses are formed according to one of three numbering name spaces managed by IEEE - MAC-48, EUI-48 and EUI-64
  • MAC-48 - used for network hardware (an obsolete term)
  • EUI-48 - used for other devices and software
  • EUI-64 - encompasses MAC-48 and EUI-48.

Unicast vs. Multicast

  • If the least significant bit of the 1st octet of a MAC address is 0 (zero), it's unicast
  • If the least significant bit of the 1st octet of a MAC address is 1 (zero), it's multicast

Universal vs. Local Addresses

  • Universally Administered Addresses can usually be distinguished from Locally Administered Addresses via the 2nd least significant bit of the 1st octet of the address.
  • This bit is known as the U/L bit
  • 0 indicates universally administered
  • 1 indicates locally adminstered

Universally Administered Addresses

  • Uniquely assigned to a device by its manufacturer
  • 1st 3 octets (bytes) identify the organization that issued the identifier. It is known as the OUI (Organizationally Unique Identifier)
  • The remainder (3 octets) of the address is assigned by the organization

Locally Administered Addresses

  • A Locally Administered Address is assigned to a device by a network administer, overriding its Universally Administered Address
