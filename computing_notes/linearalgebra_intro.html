Linear Algebra

Linear Equation

  • An algebraic equation representing a straight line when graphed in which each variable has an exponent of 1 and no variables are multipled together
  • The most common form is y = mx + b - where m is the slope and b is the y intercept

Gaussian Elimination

  • A systematic algorithm used to solve a system of linear equations (Ax = b)
  • It transforms the matrix into row echelon form (REF) - upper triangle using elementary row operations (swapping rows, multiplying a row by a scaler, adding/subtracting multiples of rows.
  • It reduces the matrix and allows you to solve using back substitution

Row Echelon Form

  • A matrix is in row echelon form (REF) if it meets the following criteria:
  • Leading entry (pivot): The first non-zero entry in a row is to the right of the leading entry in the row above it
  • Zeros Below Pivots: All entries in a column directly below a pivot are zeros
  • Zero Rows: Any rows consisting entirely of zeros are located at the bottom of the matrix

Gaussian Elimination Steps

  • Create augmented matrix. Write the coefficients and constants as [A|b] 
Given a system: 
2x + y = 5
4x - y = 1

1) Augmented Matrix
[ 2  1 | 5 ]
[ 4 -1 | 1 ]

2) REF (multiply row 1 by 2 and subtrace from row 2)
4x + 2y = 10        - multiply row 1 by 2
4x - y - 4x - 2y = -10 + 1
0x - 3y = -9

[ 2  1 | 5  ]
[ 0 -3 | -9 ]

3) Back substitution
0x - 3y = -9. y=3
2x + y = 5
2x + 3 = 5
2x = 2
x=1

answer: (1,3)
