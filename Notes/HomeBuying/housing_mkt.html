THE HOUSING MARKET

I. Housing Price Absolutes

  • Over time, housing prices rise (just like the stock market)
  • If you're planning on staying in your house for greater than 10 years, there's no need to time the purchase of your 1st house
  • How do you know if housing prices are currently overvalued? look @ rental rates.
  • Are rental rates in line with housing prices?
  • If so, housing prices are not overpriced & it's probably a good time to buy (i.e. the purchase premium of buying the house is not excessive -- usually around 20% - the smaller the better). remember, renting is fully exposed to inflation as opposed to buying w/ a fixed rate mortgage -- so over time, buying the house will be cheaper than renting and don't forget the other advantage -- you're building equity
  • If the purchase premium of buying the house is greater than 20% - the housing market is overpriced and if you're not going to own the house for 10 or more years, you should probably think twice about your purchase - you might loose money.
  • Other measures of an overheated housing market
    • home prices making front page news
    • # of real estate agents rising quickly
    • # of real estate investors rising quickly
    • if you see these characteristics - expect a correction

II. Housing Market Statistics

  • This section describes some statistics which I'd like to see readily available for the long term -- but I do not see
  • Maybe the best place to receive such statistics is your real estate agent - I've searched the internet and looked for books without success

1. Median Home Prices

  • Median home prices tell us 2 important things
    1. price trends - are housing prices in an area increasing or decreasing?
    2. price relatively - allows you compare 2 different areas/neighborhoods

2. Asking Price vs. Closing Price

  • I haven't seen anybody mention this stat (it's probably usually directly related to DOM if you examine enough houses), but I'd still like to see it

3. DOM (Days on Market)

  • Ask your real-estate agent for DOM (days on market) statistics
  • Faster property sales, the more likely they sell close to full asking price

III. Progression vs. Regression

1. Progression

  • property of lesser value is enhanced by proximity to property of greater value
  • according to the principal of progression, buy one of the cheaper houses on the block, because the more expensive houses on the block will increase the value of your house
  • remember again, this depends upon the price you're paying
  • what are other homes around it going for?
  • what's wrong with the house?
    • curable defects (i.e. cosmetic): paint job, landscaping, modernizing bathrooms, kitchens, etc.
    • incurable defects: located next to garbage dump, ugly, in need of extensive work (how much will it cost to fix up the house - is it economically feasible?)
    • difference between purchase price and value of surrounding homes defines the most you should spend on renovations

2. Regression

  • property of greater value is depreciated by proximity to property of lesser value
  • lower value of homes around yours brings down your home's value
  • most expensive house on block = worst candidate for renovations; value will never go up & you've thrown money out the window
  • TIP: if you'll end up with the most expensive house on the block when you're done with the project, DON'T DO THE PROJECT

IV. BOM (Back on Market) Properties

  • Contract falls thru due to one or more contingencies fell thru
  • When buyers & sellers couldn't agree on how to handle the corrective costs uncovered during the inspections (or buyer could not secure a mortgage)
  • BOM properties generally sell less the 2nd time around
  • buyers wonder what's wrong w/ the house - make less offers
  • sellers are usually willing to negotiate more the 2nd time around
  • Find out why it's a BOM.
  • If due to property defects, ask the sellers for copies of the inspection reports

V. Property Values

  • Always remember property value is the current value of a property.
  • The only way to accurately determine the current value for a property is to examine comps (comparisions for recent home sales in the immediate area for the past 6 months)
