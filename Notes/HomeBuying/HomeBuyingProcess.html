HOME BUYING PROCESS

I. Put Your Finances In Order

  • Track your spending for 3, 6 or 12 months
  • How much can you afford to put down for a down payment?
  • How much can you afford to pay per month?

II. Get Pre-Qualified or Pre-Approved For A Loan

  • Most real-estate agents won't show you houses until you've been at least pre-qualified for a mortgage

III. Pound the Pavement

  • Find a real-estate agent
  • Tell him/her what you're interested in
  • Search the internet, etc.
  • Most important - go physically look at the houses

IV. Make An Offer

  • Sooner or later, you'll find a house you're interested in
  • It's now time to make an offer -- which is a written, legally binding contract)
  • Have your real-estate broker produce comparision (comp) sheets for you
    • What have similar houses in the surrounding area sold for in the past 3-6 months
  • Examine the comp sheets and make a fair-value offer
  • Contracts have contingencies (conditions)
    • Financing - The deal is contingent upon you receiving the type of loan specified in the contract (i.e. 30 year fixed rate mortgage @ no more than 6% with no points w/ no more than 20% down), within the time specified in the contract (i.e. 1 month).
    • Property Inspection - The deal is contingent upon you obtaining a property inspection (within a specified amount to time - all contingencies have an associated time span) and coming to agreements with the seller on any issue(s) found.
    • Lawyer's Review of the Contract - The deal is contingent upon your lawyer's review and approval of this contract within 5 days of acceptance (again, all contingencies have a deadline)
    • A Clear Title to the Property - The deal is contingent upon the seller providing a clear title to the property
  • The seller can either
    • Accept the offer - the seller's broker will immediately give you a signed copy of your contract
    • Offer a counter offer - (another standard form) in which the seller is amending term(s) of the contract (price, etc.). You can either accept the counter offer, or offer a counter-counter offer. This can continue until both sides are in agreement

V. Escrow

  • Escrow officers (holders) are usually recommended by real-estate agents (either buyer or seller)
  • Escrow officers can be lawyers, escrow firms, or title companies
  • As soon as you and the seller have a signed contract (ratified offer), all funds and documents pertaining to the transaction should be delivered to a neutral third party (the escrow holder)
  • As soon as you open escrow, ask the escrow officer to prepare a statement of estimated closing costs
  • Escrow officers are responsible for preparing papers related to the transfer of the title, getting them properly signed, delivered, and made a matter of public record. They also order a title search

VI. Obtain A Property Inspection

  • 2/5 homes have @ least 1 major defect
  • 2 categories of defects
    • Patent defects - visible
    • Latent defects - hidden (i.e. behind walls, faulty wiring, termites, lead pipes, asbestos insulation, zoning violations, etc.)
  • Types of Inspection
    • Prepurchase Interior and Exterior Inspection
      • Usually performed by a generalist (inspector might recommend further inspection by a specialist)
      • A member of the American Society of Home Inspectors - ASHI
      • Full-time inspectors
      • How long has the company been in business
      • Errors-and-omissions insurance?
      • How many inspections / year?
      • Scope of inspection
      • How long is a typical inspection
      • Can you accompany the inspector - very important - you'll really learn the house
      • Type of Report
      • $ 200 - $ 500
      • Names/numbers of past customers
      • Ask the sellers if they have copies of any inspection reports of previous prospective buyers (if so read it and provide to your inspector)
    • Insect Inspection
      • Termites
      • Carpenter ants
      • Dry rot
      • Etc.
      • $ 75 - $ 225
  • Solving Repair Problems Uncovered By the Inspection
    • The sellers leave enough money in escrow to cover the required corrective work
    • The sellers give a credit for the corrective work @ close of escrow
    • You lower the price of the house by the amount of the repair work
    • Seller agrees to perform the corrective work

VII. Obtain Insurance

  • Title Insurance
    • A one time insurance premium due @ close of escrow
    • 90% of premium goes to research
    • 10% of premium goes to indemnifying homeowners against title claims after the close of escrow
  • Homeowners Insurance
